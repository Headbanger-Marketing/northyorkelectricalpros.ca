Before work begins

Plan the upgrades as one list, not one at a time.

Listing every planned upgrade together can let a contractor look at the whole demand on the supply instead of approving each addition in isolation. A heat pump, an induction cooktop and an EV charger, for instance, may all arrive within a year or two.

If your home shares a wall with a neighbour, the panel, meter or cable route may sit near it. Note who owns that wall and whether a condominium corporation or the neighbour must agree before any opening is made.

Should I ask about a heat pump and a charger in one enquiry?

Put every planned addition on one list with its likely timing. An assessment of the combined demand at your North York home can then show whether the existing supply is enough or whether a service change belongs in the plan.

What changes when the home shares a wall with a neighbour?

Work near a shared wall can affect the property next door. Find out who owns the wall, whether a condominium corporation has rules for openings and how any penetration will be sealed, then give those details to the contractor.

Can the upgrades be installed in stages over several years?

Staging is possible, but the first stage should not block the later ones. Ask the contractor to describe the full plan, then to state which parts of the current quote prepare for future equipment and which do not.

How do I compare quotes when each covers different upgrades?

Give each contractor an identical list of equipment and the order you expect to install it. Ask each one to separate supply changes, new circuits, notification and wall or ceiling repair, so price differences reflect scope rather than missing items.

After I send the form, who arranges a visit and the work?

North York Electrical Pros, an information site, reviews each enquiry but never sets up, prices or carries out work. Any visit is then between you and a Licensed Electrical Contractor, which agrees scope and cost, then dates; check its name and licence with ESA first.
