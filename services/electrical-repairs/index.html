A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your North York property

Record which appliances were running, which breaker tripped and how often it happens. Do not keep resetting a breaker that trips again straight away, and never open the panel cover to look.

Ask whether the circuit is overloaded, whether an appliance is faulty or whether a connection needs repair, and how each possibility will be tested. Confirm what is included before diagnostic time turns into repair work.

What should the assessment establish?

Two heavy appliances may share one circuit, or one appliance may have a fault, or a connection or the breaker itself may need attention. Whichever is found, the report names it and the evidence behind it, so a North York enquiry about a breaker that trips under combined use ends with a confirmed cause, not a guess. A new circuit may or may not be part of the answer. Where it is, the scope describes it alongside the other planned upgrades, so the fix does not use up panel space or capacity needed later for a charger or heat pump.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is it safe to keep resetting a breaker that trips with the microwave on?

A breaker that trips repeatedly is reporting a problem. Avoid resetting it again and again, stop using the combination that triggers it and arrange an assessment. If you notice heat, a burning smell or damage, leave the equipment alone.

Could fixing the tripping breaker affect my other planned upgrades?

It can. Moving an appliance to a new circuit uses panel space and part of the available supply. Mention the charger, heat pump or other equipment you plan to add so the repair is designed with that list in view.
