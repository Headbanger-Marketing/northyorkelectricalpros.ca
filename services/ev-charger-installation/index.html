EV charger installation in North York.

A charger in an attached North York garage that sits close to the panel can look like a simple job. The short route helps, but the charger still adds a large, sustained load to the home.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your North York property

Provide the vehicle and charger details, the parking position and the distance from the panel to the preferred wall. Mention any heat pump, cooktop or water heater you also plan to add.

Ask whether the existing supply can carry the charger alongside the other planned equipment, or whether load management or a service change is needed. Confirm whether the garage wall is shared and who must approve fixing equipment to it.

What should the assessment establish?

Agreeing to a charger rests on the contractor's explanation of whether it can share the supply with other planned electric equipment, or whether load management would control it. In a North York garage beside the panel, the short route has to be confirmed as suitable, but capacity is usually the harder question when other equipment is planned. The report explains what load management would mean for charging times and names the charger, its connection and the ESA notification, so later changes start from facts. If the garage shares a wall with the next unit, note any approval needed for mounting or openings.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is a short distance to the panel enough to make a charger simple?

A short route can reduce the cable and labour involved, but it says nothing about capacity. The contractor still needs to assess the charger together with existing loads and any heat pump or cooktop you plan to add.

Who has to agree before a charger goes on a shared garage wall?

If the wall is shared with a neighbour or managed by a condominium corporation, ask them before the visit. Give the contractor any written conditions so the mounting and route can be planned within them.
